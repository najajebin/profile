3 June – 27 July 2026

Neuronal and cellular basis of the egg-laying rhythm in Drosophila melanogaster

Chronobiology and Behavioural Neurogenetics Laboratory, Neuroscience Unit, JNCASR, Bengaluru.
Supervisor: Prof. Sheeba Vasu.

Status: preliminary results, unpublished.

Fruit flies lay eggs on a daily schedule set by the circadian clock. The project asked which groups of clock neurons control that timing.

I used GAL4-UAS genetics to speed up the clock in chosen neurons: all neurons, Cry-positive neurons and AstC-positive neurons. I ran egg-laying assays every two hours for 24 hours, over three days, across nine population cages, including night collections under red light. Early results point to a small set of candidate neuron groups for follow-up.

I also helped validate the MB122B split-GAL4 driver. I dissected adult brains and stained them for PER and GFP, which confirmed expression in three Cry-positive LNds and the fifth sLNv, and locomotor recordings showed an advanced evening activity peak. I practised corpora allata dissection and PER staining to test for a local clock there; I could not locate the tissue in my attempts, and worked through why. I presented the results to the lab.

Methods: fly genetics, oviposition assays, brain dissection, immunohistochemistry, fluorescence imaging, locomotor activity analysis.

Dec 2025 – Jan 2026

EEG motor imagery classification with machine learning and deep learning

Centre for Brain Research, Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru.
Supervisor: Dr. Neelam Sinha.

Status: binary task complete; five-class models in training.

The aim was to classify brain states from EEG, first on a binary mental arithmetic task and then on a five-class motor imagery dataset from PhysioNet.

Signals were cleaned with a 50 Hz notch filter, an 8–30 Hz bandpass filter and ICA artifact removal, then z-scored, cut into 4-second epochs and balanced with SMOTE. On the binary task I compared SVM, Random Forest, AdaBoost and CNN models. The best SVM reached about 96% accuracy and a simple CNN about 98.75%. The dataset was small, so I read these as a comparison of methods rather than a benchmark, and comparing a simple and a complex CNN showed how strongly dataset size limits what a model can learn.

For the five-class problem I studied hybrid 1D CNN-GRU and Bi-GRU architectures on six sensorimotor channel pairs. Training is still in progress.

Methods: signal preprocessing, scikit-learn classifiers, CNN and GRU models, cross-validation.

12 June – 22 July 2025

Management of the pulse beetle (Callosobruchus chinensis) using botanicals

Department of Zoology, Hindu College, University of Delhi (DBT Star College Scheme).
Supervisor: Dr. Anupam V. Sharma.

Status: manuscript in preparation (group project).

The pulse beetle is a major pest of stored grain. As part of a group of interns, I tested clove oil and neem oil as eco-friendly alternatives to chemical fumigants.

We set up control and treated groups with 3 to 10 replicates and recorded adult survival, egg laying, hatching and adult emergence over several weeks. Clove oil vapour caused high adult mortality and strongly reduced egg laying: under 15% of seeds carried eggs, against 46–97% in controls. Neem oil had a milder effect. I also compared host seed preference between moong and red lobia, and ran field observations showing that ants preferentially removed infested seeds and dead beetles, which suggests a role in natural pest clean-up.

Methods: insect culture, stereo-microscopy, vapour bioassays, replicated experimental design, field behavioural assays.
